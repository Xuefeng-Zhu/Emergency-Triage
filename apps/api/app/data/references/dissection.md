# Local reference: chest pain red flags / suspected dissection (dissection)

> Illustrative demo scaffolding for the triage workstation. Not a validated
> clinical protocol. The nurse and treating clinician remain the decision-makers.

§1 Tearing or ripping chest pain radiating to the back, syncope with chest
pain, or pulse/strength asymmetry between arms are red-flag findings that
warrant immediate escalation to the treating clinician.

§2 A 12-lead ECG (ECG-12) should be obtained early; it also evaluates the
common alternative diagnoses.

§3 CT angiography of the aorta (IMG-CT-AORTA) is the definitive study for
suspected dissection; it is an imaging decision for the treating clinician
and is not orderable from the triage panel.

§4 Baseline labs — complete blood count (LAB-CBC), metabolic panel (LAB-CMP),
and high-sensitivity troponin (LAB-TROP-HS) — support downstream decisions
without delaying escalation.
